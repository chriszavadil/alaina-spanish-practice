## Validation decisions

**Aymara / aimara.** The worksheet prints `el aymara`. RAE/ASALE’s Diccionario panhispánico de dudas recommends `aimara` in Spanish and discourages the y spelling. The card retains the worksheet form, explains the distinction, and accepts `el aymara`, `aymara`, `el aimara`, or `aimara`. The teacher’s expected spelling has not been confirmed. [1]

**Equatorial Guinean.** The worksheet’s `guineano / guineana` is not an error: the RAE dictionary explicitly includes Equatorial Guinea in its meaning. These displayed forms are kept. The more specific `ecuatoguineano / ecuatoguineana` is also accepted. [2, 3]

**Judge.** The displayed forms are the worksheet’s `el juez / la jueza`. The accepted alternative `la juez` is also valid according to the RAE. [4]

**Numbers and accents.** Full spellings were entered, including `dieciséis`, `veintidós`, `veintitrés`, and `veintiséis`. Numbers 16–29 are written as single words; the worksheet’s 31–49 use forms such as `treinta y uno`. [5]

**Gender forms.** Shorthand such as `fotógrafo/a` was expanded to full pronounceable forms. Nationalities were checked against the RAE/ASALE country-and-nationality reference. Forms such as `canadiense`, `estadounidense`, `costarricense`, and `nicaragüense` do not change for masculine/feminine here. Accent changes in pairs such as `alemán / alemana`, `francés / francesa`, `irlandés / irlandesa`, and `japonés / japonesa` are preserved. [6]

**Don / doña.** The worksheet’s combined example `doña Ana y don Roberto` is retained rather than replaced with señor/señora. Its English label is retained for fidelity, with a note that doña is a respectful title and does not by itself establish marital status. [7]

**Languages versus nationalities.** English language prompts explicitly include “(language)” so a card asking for French as a language is not confused with a nationality card. Language cards retain the worksheet’s masculine article. The app accepts one full form; it never asks the student to type slash shorthand.

## App grading choices, not a confirmed school rubric

The app accepts either displayed gender form, one of the displayed greeting alternatives, and leading-article omission. An included article must match an accepted phrase. Case, whitespace and final punctuation are ignored. Written accents, ñ and ü are required. Accent-only near misses receive an explanation but are not scored as fully correct. These are deliberate practice-app choices; the teacher may require articles, both gender forms, or different treatment of accents on the actual test.
