## Added examples

### Caliphate

3. In a history role-play, governors from several provinces send reports to a ruler called a caliph. He claims leadership of the Muslim community as well as authority over the territory. What kind of state are the students modeling?

Matching term: **Caliphate**. The provinces belong to a caliphate because their ruler is a caliph. The question concerns a political institution, not the name of the religion or a line of related rulers.

4. A museum labels a large territory on a map. Its explanation says that the state's ruler claimed to succeed Muhammad as leader of the Muslim community, but did not claim to be a prophet. Which government term belongs on the label?

Matching term: **Caliphate**. A state led by a caliph is a caliphate. The leadership claim identifies the institution; the ruler is not regarded as another prophet.

### Christianity

3. A visitor attends an Easter service where worshippers celebrate their belief that Jesus rose from the dead. They explain that Jesus Christ is central to their faith. Which religion does this service belong to?

Matching term: **Christianity**. The celebration of Jesus Christ's resurrection identifies Christianity. Gathering for worship alone would not distinguish it from other religions.

4. A study group reads Gospel accounts of the life of Jesus and discusses how his teachings should guide their actions. The group identifies Jesus as the Christ. Which religion are they learning about?

Matching term: **Christianity**. Christianity centers on Jesus Christ and his life and teachings. The Gospel accounts and the group's identification of Jesus provide specific clues.

### City-state

3. An independent harbor city sends its own representative to negotiate with neighboring countries. Its government rules the city and a small stretch of nearby farmland, with no higher national government above it. What is this political unit?

Matching term: **City-state**. This is a city-state: a city and the nearby territory it governs independently. Having a harbor is not the defining feature; governing itself as a state is.

4. On a fictional map, one border encloses a single city and the villages that supply it with food. The city sets laws for this territory and is independent of the kingdoms around it. Which term best describes the whole territory?

Matching term: **City-state**. The independent city and its surrounding land together form a city-state. The nearby villages do not change the city's role as the center of an independent state.

### Caravan

3. Mountain traders agree to travel together with mules carrying their merchandise. They share a guide and move as one group between distant markets. What term describes the travelers and animals, rather than the path they follow?

Matching term: **Caravan**. The traveling group is a caravan. Pack animals can be mules as well as camels; the important clue is people and their loads making a journey together.

4. At an oasis, several trading families refill their water containers and check the bundles on their animals. After resting, the entire group sets off together for the next trading town. What is this traveling group called?

Matching term: **Caravan**. The families and their loaded animals form a caravan. An oasis is a stopping place, while the caravan is the group that moves from place to place.

### Feudalism

3. In an imagined medieval agreement, a noble grants a follower the right to hold land. The follower swears loyalty and promises to bring armed supporters when the noble calls. Which system of relationships does the agreement illustrate?

Matching term: **Feudalism**. The land-for-loyalty-and-service arrangement illustrates feudalism in the simplified classroom model. It is the bargain between lord and vassal, not inheritance alone, that matters.

4. A messenger asks a medieval vassal to fulfill a promise of military service. The vassal explains that this duty comes with the land rights received from a lord, rather than an ordinary paid job. Which concept connects the land rights and obligation?

Matching term: **Feudalism**. Feudalism describes relationships linking grants of land or rights to loyalty and service. Medieval practices varied, but this example uses the core lord-vassal exchange.

### Silk Road

3. For a class project, students trace how textiles and new ideas could pass through many markets from East Asia toward the Middle East and Europe. No single merchant needs to travel the entire distance. Which historical trade network are they modeling?

Matching term: **Silk Road**. The Silk Road was a connected network in which goods and ideas could pass from trader to trader. It was not one road that every merchant traveled from end to end.

4. A guide links inland trading towns and sea routes on a map stretching across Asia toward Europe and Africa. The guide discusses exchanges of beliefs, skills, and merchandise along these connections. Which broad historical network is the focus?

Matching term: **Silk Road**. The Silk Road, also called the Silk Roads, included connected land and maritime routes. Its importance involved cultural exchange as well as the movement of goods.

### Black Plague

3. In a classroom simulation set in 1348, a town's records describe many deaths during a plague outbreak. Reports from distant towns show it is part of a much larger pandemic. Which historical event provides the setting?

Matching term: **Black Plague**. The date and the widespread medieval plague pandemic point to the Black Plague, also called the Black Death. This imagined town is a learning example, not a quotation from a real record.

4. A student makes a timeline of the 1300s and marks a plague pandemic that devastated communities across parts of Europe, Asia, and North Africa. The event is much larger than one village's illness outbreak. Which label belongs on the timeline?

Matching term: **Black Plague**. The fourteenth-century pandemic is the Black Plague or Black Death. Its time period and broad geographic reach distinguish it from other disease outbreaks.

### Daoism

3. A learner is frustrated by trying to control every detail of life. An instructor in a Chinese tradition recommends studying the Way and learning to act in harmony with the natural order instead of forcing every outcome. Which tradition fits this advice?

Matching term: **Daoism**. Daoism emphasizes harmony with the Dao, or Way. The advice illustrates working with the natural order; it does not mean that followers must never act or make decisions.

4. In a discussion of Laozi's ideas, a student compares a thoughtful life to a garden tended with the seasons rather than against them. The student connects the example to following the Way. Which Chinese tradition is being illustrated?

Matching term: **Daoism**. Laozi and the Way are clues to Daoism, also spelled Taoism. The garden is an original analogy for harmony with nature, not a historical quotation or a rule about gardening.

### Islam

3. A visitor to a mosque hears people recite from the Quran. The community explains that they worship one God and regard Muhammad as God's final prophet. Which religion are they describing?

Matching term: **Islam**. The Quran, worship of one God, and Muhammad's role as final prophet identify Islam. Muslims worship God, not Muhammad.

4. A student learns about the Five Pillars, including prayer, giving to those in need, and fasting during Ramadan. The lesson explains that these practices express the faith of Muslims. Which religion is the lesson about?

Matching term: **Islam**. The Five Pillars are foundational practices of Islam. The reference to Muslims and Ramadan distinguishes this religion from other traditions that also value prayer and generosity.

### Luxury Goods

3. A buyer can drink from a simple cup but chooses a costly goblet decorated with precious stones to impress guests. What category best describes the goblet in this situation, rather than an item needed for survival?

Matching term: **Luxury Goods**. The decorated goblet is a luxury good because the buyer values its beauty and status beyond a basic need. Its purpose in this situation matters, not simply that it is an object for sale.

4. After buying enough basic food and clothing, a wealthy household orders rare ornaments and finely woven wall hangings to display its status. Which term describes these extra purchases?

Matching term: **Luxury Goods**. These are luxury goods: nonessential items prized for beauty, rarity, or status. The example separates them from basic necessities the household already has.

### Jihad

3. A Muslim community describes its demanding, peaceful effort to help people in need as striving in the path of God. The question asks for the term for that religious striving, not the name of the religion itself. Which term fits?

Matching term: **Jihad**. Jihad can refer to striving in the path of God, including moral and spiritual effort. The religious purpose is the key clue here; the word is not simply another name for Islam.

4. A student encounters a word in two readings about Islam. In one it describes personal spiritual striving; in a historical discussion it can refer to armed struggle. The teacher says to read the context instead of translating it only as war. Which word is being studied?

Matching term: **Jihad**. Jihad has meanings of striving or struggle that depend on context, including spiritual effort and armed struggle in historical and legal discussions. It should not be reduced to just one of those meanings.

### Dynasty

3. A kingdom's ruler is followed by her niece, then by another relative from the same family. A historian groups these successive rulers together because of their shared family line. What is that line of rulers called?

Matching term: **Dynasty**. A dynasty is a succession of rulers from the same family or lineage. The rulers do not have to be a chain of fathers and sons to belong to the same dynasty.

4. In a fictional kingdom, one ruling family's line ends. A different family takes power, and several generations of its relatives later rule. What term describes the new family's succession of rulers?

Matching term: **Dynasty**. The new succession of related rulers forms a dynasty. The concept identifies a ruling lineage, rather than a single ruler or a system for selecting people by skill.

### Confucianism

3. A class discusses a teaching associated with Confucius: a good ruler should model moral behavior and take responsibilities to others seriously. Students connect good government with ethical conduct. Which tradition are they studying?

Matching term: **Confucianism**. Confucianism links moral behavior, responsibilities in relationships, and good social order. Naming Confucius makes the connection more specific than a general appeal to kindness.

4. A teacher uses Confucius's ideas to discuss learning, caring for parents, and treating others responsibly. The class considers how fulfilling these duties can support harmony in family and society. Which tradition guides the lesson?

Matching term: **Confucianism**. Education, family responsibilities, and ethical relationships are important in Confucianism. The lesson is about a tradition of conduct and social duties, not simply a method of hiring officials.

### Meritocracy

3. A scholarship panel removes applicants' names and family connections from its review. It compares demonstrated skills and achievements instead of giving preference to wealthy or influential families. Which principle is the panel trying to apply?

Matching term: **Meritocracy**. The panel is applying the ideal of meritocracy by using ability and achievement rather than inherited status or connections. This describes its selection principle, not proof that every applicant had equal opportunities.

4. Two people apply for a leadership role. One is related to an important official; the other performs better on the relevant skills assessment. The committee selects the stronger performer without favoring the family connection. Which principle does that illustrate?

Matching term: **Meritocracy**. Meritocracy rewards demonstrated ability and achievement rather than personal connections. The skill-based selection, not the mere presence of a committee, is the important clue.

### Buddhism

3. A learner reads the Buddha's teachings and asks how people can respond to suffering through ethical choices and wisdom. The discussion focuses on a path toward liberation rather than gaining wealth or political power. Which tradition is the learner studying?

Matching term: **Buddhism**. Buddhism is based on the Buddha's teachings about suffering and liberation. Ethical living and wisdom are clues alongside the explicit reference to the Buddha.

4. At a study center, a teacher explains that meditation is one part of practicing the Buddha's teachings. Students also discuss ethical behavior and understanding suffering. Which religious and philosophical tradition connects these activities?

Matching term: **Buddhism**. The Buddha's teachings connect these practices to Buddhism. Meditation by itself is not unique to this tradition, so the reference to the Buddha and suffering is essential.
